# Incident Response Reports

## What this demonstrates

Formal incident response documentation, a distinct SOC skill from the other five build-and-detect projects in this portfolio. Each report follows NIST SP 800-61 Rev 3, *Incident Response Recommendations and Considerations for Cybersecurity Risk Management: A CSF 2.0 Community Profile*, and is organized around the three CSF 2.0 Functions that Rev 3 places within incident response itself: Detect, Respond, and Recover. Each has a timeline built from real evidence, indicators of compromise, MITRE ATT&CK mapping, a severity and escalation decision with stated reasoning, containment and remediation actions, and root cause analysis, and each is explicit about what the evidence does and doesn't support rather than forcing a conclusion.

Both incidents were staged deliberately against this lab's own infrastructure, and each report states its premise up front. What is real is the evidence: the timestamps, event records, hashes, and alerts were generated by the exercise itself, not written from imagination. Identifying values are replaced or redacted. Where a step cannot be performed in a single-operator lab, such as out-of-band verification with an account owner, the report says so and reasons about it hypothetically rather than claiming it happened.

## Key findings

Three things these two investigations established.

**Attribution to a legitimate account is not confirmation of legitimate intent.** Incident-01's account creation and privilege escalation trace to a known local administrator. A compromised administrator session produces identical evidence, and that ambiguity is why the incident escalates instead of closing at Tier 1.

**An attempt to destroy evidence can be defeated by the layer that detects it.** The operator in incident-02 truncated the authentication log to 106 bytes (one line). The File Integrity Monitoring alert had already captured the file's complete prior content in its diff field, so the live copy was destroyed but the record survived, and the alert became the authoritative source for what was erased.

**A report that will not force a conclusion is worth more than one that does.** Incident-01 leaves its root cause unresolved and says so, holding two hypotheses open rather than asserting the more dramatic one. Incident-02's root cause is out of scope by the exercise's own premise, and the report says so instead of presenting an evidence gap as a finding.

## What ties these together

Each incident is scoped around one point in an attack chain rather than a full end-to-end compromise. Incident-01 investigates a completed privilege escalation and reasons about what the evidence alone can and can't say about intent. Incident-02 investigates an anti-forensic action by an actor who already holds access, and documents what it took to make that action detectable — including a documented Wazuh behavior ([issue #32426](https://github.com/wazuh/wazuh/issues/32426), which the maintainers closed as working by design) that had to be worked around before detection worked.
